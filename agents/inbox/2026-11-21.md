# Tech scout inbox, 2026-11-21

Gap fillers. None of these names appear in `src/data` or in the two unmerged 2026-11-20 inbox files. The four storage and display interfaces assume the "Buses and interconnects" group proposed in `2026-11-20-2.md`; each names a fallback parent that exists today.

## X Window System
- year: 1984
- kind: os
- era: personal
- parent: unix (fallback: wimp)
- creators: Bob Scheifler, Jim Gettys
- wikipedia: X Window System
- summary: The network transparent windowing system of MIT's Project Athena, grown from Stanford's W. Its protocol has been at version 11 since September 1987, which is why most people call it X11.
- sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X_Window_System , https://www.x.org/wiki/
- confidence: high

## SCSI
- year: 1986
- kind: component
- era: personal
- parent: Buses and interconnects group (fallback: hard-disk)
- creators: Larry Boucher (SASI at Shugart Associates); standardised by the ANSI X3T9 committee
- wikipedia: SCSI
- summary: A command set and bus for attaching disks, tapes and scanners, standardised as SCSI-1 in 1986 from Shugart's 1981 SASI. It ruled servers and workstations, and its commands live on in SAS and USB storage.
- sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SCSI
- confidence: high

## Serial ATA
- year: 2003
- kind: component
- era: modern
- parent: Buses and interconnects group (fallback: hard-disk)
- creators: none (Serial ATA Working Group, now SATA-IO)
- wikipedia: SATA
- summary: A thin seven wire serial link that replaced the wide parallel ATA ribbon cable for disks and optical drives. Announced in 2000, revision 1.0 and the first drive, Seagate's Barracuda SATA V, came in January 2003.
- sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SATA , https://www.sata-io.org/
- confidence: high

## FireWire (IEEE 1394)
- year: 1995
- kind: component
- era: personal
- parent: Buses and interconnects group (fallback: macintosh)
- creators: Apple, with Sony and Panasonic
- wikipedia: IEEE 1394
- summary: Apple's fast serial bus, completed as IEEE 1394-1995, that carried video from camcorders in real time without a host controller. USB, which came later, won the mass market.
- sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_1394
- confidence: high

## HDMI
- year: 2003
- kind: component
- era: modern
- parent: displays (or the Buses and interconnects group)
- creators: Hitachi, Panasonic, Philips, Silicon Image, Sony, Thomson and Toshiba
- wikipedia: HDMI
- summary: One digital cable for picture and sound between players, consoles, computers and screens, built to stay compatible with DVI. Work on 1.0 began in April 2002 and it reached products in 2003, replacing composite, S-Video and VGA.
- sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HDMI , https://www.hdmi.org/about
- confidence: high

## Thunderbolt
- year: 2011
- kind: component
- era: modern
- parent: Buses and interconnects group (PCI Express, if added; fallback: peripherals)
- creators: Intel, with Apple (developed as Light Peak)
- wikipedia: Thunderbolt (interface)
- summary: PCI Express and DisplayPort carried together on one cable with power, first sold in the MacBook Pro on 24 February 2011. From version 3 it moved to the USB-C connector.
- sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thunderbolt_(interface) , https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2011/02/24Apple-Updates-MacBook-Pro-with-Next-Generation-Processors-Graphics-Thunderbolt-I-O-Technology/
- confidence: high

## Turing test
- year: 1950
- kind: concept
- era: theory
- parent: ai (fallback: turing-machine)
- creators: Alan Turing
- wikipedia: Turing test
- summary: Turing's imitation game from "Computing Machinery and Intelligence" in Mind, October 1950. It swapped the question "can machines think" for whether a judge can tell a machine from a person in conversation.
- sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turing_test , https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1093/mind/LIX.236.433
- confidence: high

## ELIZA
- year: 1966
- kind: concept
- era: integrated
- parent: ai (or under Turing test, if added)
- creators: Joseph Weizenbaum
- wikipedia: ELIZA
- summary: An early chatbot written at MIT from 1964 to 1967, best known for its DOCTOR script that played a therapist by reflecting the user's words back. People who knew it was a program still confided in it, which became known as the ELIZA effect.
- sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ELIZA , https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1145/365153.365168
- confidence: high

## File Transfer Protocol
- year: 1971
- kind: network
- era: network
- parent: arpanet
- creators: Abhay Bhushan
- wikipedia: File Transfer Protocol
- summary: Specified by Abhay Bhushan in RFC 114 in April 1971, one of the oldest application protocols still in use. It moved to TCP/IP in 1980 and its current form, RFC 959, dates from 1985.
- sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File_Transfer_Protocol , https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc114.txt , https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc959.txt
- confidence: high

## Kerberos
- year: 1989
- kind: network
- era: network
- parent: symmetric (fallback: systems-security)
- creators: Steve Miller, Clifford Neuman (version 5 with John Kohl)
- wikipedia: Kerberos (protocol)
- summary: Ticket based network login built at MIT for Project Athena on the Needham and Schroeder protocol. Work began in 1986, version 4 was released publicly in January 1989, and version 5 underpins Windows domain sign in.
- sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerberos_(protocol) , https://web.mit.edu/kerberos/
- confidence: high

## Secure Shell (SSH)
- year: 1995
- kind: network
- era: network
- parent: public-key (fallback: systems-security)
- creators: Tatu Ylonen
- wikipedia: Secure Shell
- summary: Encrypted remote login designed in 1995 by Tatu Ylonen to replace Telnet, rsh and rlogin, which sent passwords in the clear. It became the default way to reach a remote machine and was standardised by the IETF in 2006.
- sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secure_Shell , https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc4251.txt
- confidence: high

## Siri
- year: 2011
- kind: concept
- era: modern
- parent: iphone (fallback: ai)
- creators: none (spun off from SRI International's AI Center; acquired by Apple)
- wikipedia: Siri
- summary: A voice assistant that SRI International spun off in 2007. It was sold as a free iPhone app, bought by Apple in April 2010 and built into the iPhone 4S in October 2011. It made talking to a phone ordinary and was followed by Google Now, Cortana and Alexa.
- sources: https://www.sri.com/hoi/siri/ , https://www.iclarified.com/entry/index.php?enid=9134 , https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2011/10/04Apple-Launches-iPhone-4S-iOS-5-iCloud/
- note: corrected after review. Wikipedia's Siri article gives December 2010 for the app and contradicts itself on the acquisition date; SRI says Apple acquired Siri in April 2010, and iClarified reported the acquisition on 28 April 2010 while the app was already in the App Store. The app's exact launch month is left out.
- confidence: high
