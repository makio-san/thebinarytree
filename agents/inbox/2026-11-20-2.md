# Tech scout inbox, 2026-11-20 (second run)

## Universal Serial Bus (USB)
- year: 1996
- kind: component
- era: modern
- parent: peripherals (no bus or interconnect group exists yet; the curator may want a small "Buses and interconnects" group under peripherals, holding USB, PCI, PCI Express and CXL)
- creators: Ajay Bhatt and his team at Intel; specification by Compaq, DEC, IBM, Intel, Microsoft, NEC and Nortel
- wikipedia: USB
- summary: One plug and one protocol for keyboards, mice, printers and drives, with power on the same cable. USB 1.0 was finalised in January 1996 and replaced a drawer full of serial, parallel and game ports.
- sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USB , https://ethw.org/Milestones:Universal_Serial_Bus_(USB),_1996 , https://heise.de/-11142623
- confidence: high

## Peripheral Component Interconnect (PCI)
- year: 1992
- kind: component
- era: personal
- parent: peripherals (same proposed group as USB)
- creators: Intel
- wikipedia: Peripheral Component Interconnect
- summary: Intel's local bus for expansion cards, independent of any one processor. It replaced ISA and VESA slots in PCs and Macs alike and stayed the standard card slot for over a decade.
- sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peripheral_Component_Interconnect
- confidence: high

## PCI Express
- year: 2003
- kind: component
- era: modern
- parent: PCI (if added), else peripherals
- creators: Intel, Dell, HP and IBM (developed as 3GIO, published by PCI-SIG)
- wikipedia: PCI Express
- summary: PCI rebuilt as point to point serial lanes instead of a shared parallel bus. It replaced PCI, PCI-X and AGP and now carries graphics cards, SSDs and network cards in nearly every computer.
- sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PCI_Express , https://www.intel.com/pressroom/archive/releases/2002/20020417net.htm , https://convergedigest.com/pci-sig-finalizes-pcie-7-0-specification-at-128-0-gt-s/
- confidence: high (note: draft 1.0 was announced April 2002 and dated July 2002; 1.0a, the version products shipped against, is April 2003, which Wikipedia uses. PCIe 7.0 was finalised June 2025 and could be a fact.)

## Compute Express Link (CXL)
- year: 2019
- kind: component
- era: modern
- parent: PCI Express (if added), else peripherals
- creators: Intel (contributed to a consortium founded by Alibaba, Cisco, Dell EMC, Facebook, Google, HPE, Huawei, Intel and Microsoft)
- wikipedia: Compute Express Link
- summary: A cache coherent link built on the PCI Express physical layer, letting processors share memory with accelerators and attach pooled memory outside the socket. Version 1.0 was published in March 2019.
- sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compute_Express_Link , https://www.anandtech.com/show/14068 , https://www.hpcwire.com/2019/03/14/cxl-consortium-launches-cpu-to-anything-high-speed-interconnect-protocol/
- confidence: high

## Gate-all-around transistor (GAAFET)
- year: 2022
- kind: component
- era: modern
- parent: finfet
- creators: none (Samsung was first to production; sources credit the company)
- wikipedia: Multigate device
- summary: The gate wraps the channel on all four sides, using stacked nanosheets instead of a fin. Samsung started production on its 3 nm node with it in June 2022, and Intel's 18A followed with RibbonFET plus backside power delivery in the Core Ultra Series 3 laptops of January 2026.
- sources: https://news.samsung.com/us/samsung-begins-chip-production-using-3nm-process-technology-with-gaa-architecture , https://www.anandtech.com/show/17474 , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multigate_device , https://newsroom.intel.com/client-computing/ces-2026-intel-core-ultra-series-3-debut-first-built-on-intel-18a
- confidence: high (Samsung's 2022 output was "initial" production; volume came later. The summary should say started production, not mass production.)

## JUPITER
- year: 2025
- kind: machine
- era: modern
- parent: supercomputers
- creators: none (Forschungszentrum Jülich and EuroHPC JU; built by Eviden with NVIDIA)
- wikipedia: Jupiter (supercomputer)
- summary: Europe's first exascale supercomputer and the first outside the United States, inaugurated at Jülich in September 2025. Its booster uses about 24,000 NVIDIA GH200 Grace Hopper superchips.
- sources: https://eurohpc-ju.europa.eu/jupiter-launching-europes-exascale-era-2025-09-05_en , https://www.theregister.com/2025/09/05/jupiter_supercompute_exascale_threshold/
- confidence: high

## Quantinuum Helios
- year: 2025
- kind: machine
- era: modern
- parent: quantum
- creators: none (Quantinuum)
- wikipedia: Quantinuum (no article for Helios itself)
- summary: A 98 qubit trapped-ion quantum computer launched in November 2025, using barium ions moved through a junction. The tree's quantum line is all superconducting so far; this adds the trapped-ion line and the highest gate fidelities reported for a commercial machine.
- sources: https://quantinuum.com/blog/introducing-helios-the-most-accurate-quantum-computer-in-the-world , https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.05465v1 , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantinuum
- confidence: medium (fidelity figures are the vendor's own; the curator may prefer a node for trapped-ion quantum computing in general with Helios as a fact)

## Free-threaded CPython
- year: 2024
- kind: concept
- era: modern
- parent: python
- creators: Sam Gross
- wikipedia: Global interpreter lock (no dedicated article)
- summary: A build of CPython without the global interpreter lock, so Python threads can run on several cores at once. It shipped as experimental in Python 3.13 in October 2024 and became officially supported in later releases.
- sources: https://peps.python.org/pep-0703/ , https://docs.python.org/3.13/whatsnew/3.13.html , https://www.python.org/downloads/release/python-3130/ , https://docs.python.org/3.15/howto/free-threading-python.html
- confidence: high
