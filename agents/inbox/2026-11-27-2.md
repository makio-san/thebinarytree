## MVS
- year: 1974
- kind: os
- era: micro
- parent: os-360
- creators: IBM
- wikipedia: MVS
- summary: Multiple Virtual Storage, the IBM mainframe operating system that succeeded OS/360 on the System/370 and later machines. It became the most widely used mainframe system and the base that z/OS grew from.
- sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MVS
- confidence: high

## PL/I
- year: 1964
- kind: language
- era: integrated
- parent: algol-60
- creators: IBM, SHARE Language Development Committee
- wikipedia: PL/I
- summary: IBM's attempt at one language for both scientific and business work, designed loosely on ALGOL and borrowing from Fortran and COBOL. It never displaced either, but it stayed in use on mainframes for decades.
- sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PL/I
- confidence: high

## Oberon
- year: 1987
- kind: language
- era: personal
- parent: modula-2
- creators: Niklaus Wirth
- wikipedia: Oberon (programming language)
- summary: Niklaus Wirth's successor to Modula-2, smaller yet more powerful, whose main new idea was extending record types. It was written together with an operating system of the same name at ETH Zurich.
- sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberon_(programming_language)
- confidence: high

## Hack
- year: 1984
- kind: concept
- era: personal
- parent: rogue
- creators: Jay Fenlason, Kenny Woodland, Mike Thome, Jonathan Payne, Andries Brouwer
- wikipedia: Hack (video game)
- summary: A roguelike begun in 1982 by Jay Fenlason and school friends and released on Usenet in a much extended version by Andries Brouwer in 1984. It added shops and many more monsters, items and spells, and became the basis for NetHack.
- sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hack_(video_game)
- confidence: high

## NetHack
- year: 1987
- kind: concept
- era: personal
- parent: Hack (queued above), else rogue
- creators: Mike Stephenson, NetHack Development Team
- wikipedia: NetHack
- summary: A fork of Hack first posted on 28 July 1987 and developed ever since by a team working over the internet. It is one of the oldest games still in active development and influenced later games such as Diablo.
- sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NetHack
- confidence: high

## SHA-1
- year: 1995
- kind: concept
- era: modern
- parent: integrity
- creators: National Security Agency
- wikipedia: SHA-1
- summary: The 160 bit hash published by NIST in FIPS 180-1 in 1995, revising a 1993 version the NSA withdrew. It was everywhere for two decades, then broken in practice, and is being phased out in favour of SHA-2 and SHA-3.
- sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SHA-1
- confidence: high

## Multiprotocol Label Switching (MPLS)
- year: 2001
- kind: network
- era: network
- parent: tcp-ip
- creators: IETF MPLS Working Group (from Cisco Tag Switching and Ipsilon IP Switching)
- wikipedia: Multiprotocol Label Switching
- summary: Forwarding packets along set paths by short labels instead of looking up the address at every hop. Standardised in RFC 3031 in 2001, it now carries most carrier traffic engineering and business VPNs.
- sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiprotocol_Label_Switching
- confidence: medium

## Mamba
- year: 2023
- kind: concept
- era: modern
- parent: transformer
- creators: Albert Gu, Tri Dao
- wikipedia: Mamba (deep learning architecture)
- summary: A sequence model built on selective state spaces that runs in linear time with sequence length, proposed in December 2023 as an alternative to attention for long inputs. It started a family of state space and hybrid language models.
- sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamba_(deep_learning_architecture), https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00752
- confidence: high
