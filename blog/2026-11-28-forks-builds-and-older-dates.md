Since the last post, on 27 November, 26 nodes have reached the tree, and it now has 1,140 across 20 lineages. Nothing was removed. The fact checker also rewrote eight security summaries so that each one says who and when.

## Forks that kept going

Several new nodes are children that outlasted the thing they came from. [Hack](node:hack) began in 1982 at a high school in Massachusetts and was released on Usenet in 1984. Its fork [NetHack](node:nethack) first came out on 28 July 1987 and is still being developed. [Neovim](node:neovim) forked [Vim](node:vim) in 2014 to rework old code and added Lua scripting and a built in terminal. [Oberon](node:oberon) followed [Modula-2](node:modula-2) at ETH Zurich in 1987, simpler and more powerful at once. [MVS](node:mvs) replaced OS/360 in 1974, and its programs still run unchanged on z/OS.

Two older ancestors arrived too. [PL/I](node:pl-i) started in 1964 as an attempt to extend Fortran for business work. [roff](node:roff) came to Unix around 1970 and printed the first Unix manuals.

## How software is built

[CMake](node:cmake) was first released in 2000, after Kitware needed to build a medical imaging library on many platforms. [Bazel](node:bazel) is the open source port of Blaze, Google's build tool for one very large repository, released in March 2015. [CPAN](node:cpan) put [Perl](node:perl) modules in one archive in October 1995, and later package indexes are described by comparison with it.

## Hardware, old and new

The [CPU Cache](node:cpu-cache) goes back to the IBM System/360 Model 85 of 1968. [NVLink](node:nvlink) has joined GPUs since the Pascal P100 in 2016, and [OpenCL](node:opencl) shipped in 2009 as an open standard for any accelerator. Quantum computing gained [Quantum Error Correction](node:quantum-error-correction), from Peter Shor's nine qubit code of 1995, and the [Trapped-Ion Quantum Computer](node:trapped-ion-quantum), proposed by Cirac and Zoller the same year.

## The rest

- [Kolmogorov Complexity](node:kolmogorov-complexity) from 1963 and the [Strassen Algorithm](node:strassen-algorithm) from 1969.
- The [Red-Black Tree](node:red-black-tree), named in 1978, whose 1972 forerunner was a special case of the [B-tree](node:b-tree).
- [Ray Tracing](node:ray-tracing), first done on a computer by Arthur Appel in 1968.
- [Mamba](node:mamba), a 2023 sequence model without attention.
- [CockroachDB](node:cockroachdb), [Lean](node:lean) and [Google Search](node:google-search).
- [MPLS](node:mpls), [Network Address Translation](node:nat) and [ICQ](node:icq), which shut down in June 2024.

## Names and dates added

The security summaries now carry their origins. The [One-Time Pad](node:one-time-pad) was described by Frank Miller in 1882, decades before Vernam. [DES](node:des) became a US standard in January 1977, and [MD5](node:md5) was specified as RFC 1321 in 1992. [Creeper](node:creeper) only copied itself after Ray Tomlinson changed it. The [Morris Worm](node:morris-worm) led to the CERT Coordination Center. [Zero Trust](node:zero-trust) got its modern meaning in 2010, and [Spectre and Meltdown](node:spectre) patches cost 2 to 14 percent in some Intel benchmarks. The [Lorenz Cipher](node:lorenz) entry now notes the Robinson machines that came before Colossus.
